# Visual Direction: RallyLoop Landing Page

**World:** Nike product campaign landing page
**Mode:** Persuade
**First surface:** Landing page (/)

## World: Nike Product Campaign

Nike's product campaign landing pages combine bold athletic photography with minimal, high-impact UI overlays. The page feels premium, aspirational, and driven by motion. Typography is large and confident—often custom or very strong sans-serif faces set at display sizes. Imagery shows athletes in action, mid-leap, or peak performance moments. The UI gets out of the way: thin navigation, minimal framing, and clear calls-to-action that don't compete with the hero. Colors are deliberate—bold black and white with a strong accent (often volt green, university red, or hyper blue) used sparingly for primary actions and state changes. Animations are sophisticated: scroll-triggered reveals, parallax depth, and motion that mirrors athletic energy without feeling gratuitous. The page sells an aspirational lifestyle: this product helps you perform, this is what athletes use, this is how champions are made.

## First Viewport

The hero is full-bleed athletic photography: a pickleball, badminton, or tennis player captured mid-action—racquet back, knees bent, eyes focused on the ball. The image bleeds to the edges. Overlaid on the left is the RallyLoop wordmark in a strong, condensed display type, white with a subtle shadow for readability against the photo. Below it, a single-line headline in bold sans-serif: "Every Game. One Queue." On the right, vertically centered, is the primary action: a tall, full-height "Join RallyLoop" button in the accent color (RallyLoop green) with white text, no border, subtle hover lift. The button has a micro-interaction: a quick scale-down-then-up on hover that feels like a starting motion. Below the hero image, a thin horizontal rule extends to the edges, and a badge row appears on hover: "Free for players · Live queue tracking · Real-time court updates."

## Visitor Path

The visitor lands on the hero and immediately sees the offer: athletic energy, the promise (queue management), and the action (sign up). Scrolling down, the page explains RallyLoop through three asymmetric panels. First, a "How it works" section with numbered steps rendered as bold icons with one-line copy: "1. Find a session, 2. Join the queue, 3. Play when assigned." Second, a "For Facilities" section targeting staff, with a shorter headline and a button to "Request access for your facility." Third, a "Sports we support" grid showing pickleball, badminton, tennis, and more with icon and name. Each section uses the same structural language: bold headline in the condensed display type, left-aligned, with supporting copy in sans-serif below. Panels are separated by generous whitespace, not borders—air and photography carry the rhythm. The primary CTA (sign up) is always accessible via a sticky header that appears on scroll: RallyLoop wordmark left, a "Join" button right, minimal. The page ends with a final motivational panel: "Your next game is waiting. Join RallyLoop today." and the sign-up form.

## Signature Interaction

The primary "Join RallyLoop" button has an athletic micro-interaction: on hover, it scales down slightly (0.95) then up (1.05) with a smooth cubic-bezier curve, like a muscle preparing to fire. Clicking triggers a ripple that expands from the click point, then the button smoothly transforms into a loading state with a circular spinner in the accent color. Success animations are quick and decisive: the button flashes white, then transitions to a checkmark icon, then the form slides in from the right with a smooth translate. Scroll-triggered animations are athletic: elements slide up with a staggered delay (100ms, 200ms, 300ms) like runners starting in lanes, rather than fading in. Images have a subtle parallax effect when the user scrolls, creating depth and dynamism.

## Cross-Surface Reach

This landing page's visual language extends into the app shell and authentication pages. The bold condensed display type for headlines becomes the RallyLoop type system's display tier. The accent green becomes the primary action color across the product: buttons, active states, and key highlights. The generous whitespace and minimal borders established here carry into the admin and player interfaces. The athletic micro-interactions (hover scale, ripple, staggered animations) become a motion system that defines RallyLoop's digital feel—confident, responsive, energetic without being chaotic. Photography, when added later, should follow the same principles: action shots, peak moments, athletes in motion, not static studio portraits.

## Honest Risk

The hero photography requirement is the biggest risk. Without real court-sport photography, we risk using generic stock imagery that dilutes the athletic authenticity the Nike world demands. The fallback plan: use dynamic CSS-based court illustrations (pickleball court patterns, badminton net lines, tennis court geometry) with animated elements (a ball bouncing across the court, a racquet swinging in a loop) while we commission real photography. This preserves the motion and athletic character without compromising authenticity with placeholder photos.

## Raiser from WebGL Shader Portal

Raise from webgl-shader-portal: a commitment to advanced motion and cinematic feel. The shader portal's philosophy of living pixels and smooth transitions inspires RallyLoop's scroll-triggered animations, parallax effects, and interaction micro-animations. We borrow its discipline: motion should feel purposeful and tied to the product's logic (queue movement, court assignment, match transitions), not decorative for its own sake.

## Verdict on Challenger: Brick Build Instructions

Declined. The brick instruction world's step-by-step, grid-aligned structure is too rigid for an athletic brand aesthetic. RallyLoop is about fluid queue movement and real-time play, not a rigid assembly process. The assigned Nike direction better captures the energy and aspirational quality of sports.

## Verdict on Challenger: Film Cutting Bench

Declined. The cutting bench's industrial, frame-by-frame metaphor is too mechanical for a sports-focused landing page. RallyLoop is about live action and continuous play, not linear editing. The Nike direction's motion and energy better match the product's real-time queue management.

## Verdict on Challenger: Convention Circle Catalog

Declined. The catalog's dense grid and stamp-sized thumbnails would reduce RallyLoop's value proposition to a commodity listing. The Nike direction's hero-first, aspiration-focused approach better positions RallyLoop as a premium sports platform, not a directory.

## Verdict on Challenger: Martial Arts Film Hoarding

Declined. The wuxia hoarding's single static frame and cinematic stillness conflicts with RallyLoop's real-time, dynamic nature. The Nike direction's commitment to motion, scroll interactions, and athletic energy better serves a live queue management product.

## Verdict on Challenger: Ikebana Alcove

Declined. The ikebana world's emphasis on quiet emptiness and seasonal restraint is the opposite of the bold, energetic sports aesthetic the user requested. The Nike direction's confidence, motion, and aspirational energy align with "professional and sporty" better than a contemplative Japanese garden metaphor.

## Verdict on Challenger: WebGL Shader Portal

Competitive. The shader portal's full-screen living pixels and immersive motion are compelling for an experience-focused product. However, for a Persuade-mode landing page with a sign-up action, the Nike direction's clearer hierarchy, more conventional information architecture, and photograph-driven hero better serve conversion. The shader portal's ambition is noted: if RallyLoop expands into an immersive player experience (VR court visualization, live match streams), that world becomes a strong candidate for that surface.

Code-led. Image generation is not available, so we build with the direction contract as our guide: typography, color strategy, layout skeleton, motion system, and interaction design, all derived from the Nike product campaign world.
